# Prior art

The map. Each row says what a paper actually established and, more importantly
for this project, **what it did not measure** — because the gap this repository
occupies is made entirely of things adjacent papers left out.

`status` is `verified` only when the paper's own text has been read through
`scripts/extract_paper.py` and the row's claim located in it. `abstract-only`
means exactly that, and an `abstract-only` row may not be cited as evidence
that something was not done: abstracts omit non-findings by construction.

An unverified row is not a defect. An unmarked one is.

Last full sweep: see [SWEEPS.md](SWEEPS.md).

The same relationship as the block below: settled science, used to measure, not

## Population stability — the measuring instrument, not the subject

This block is settled science that the practice ignores. It is not where the
contribution is; it is what the contribution is measured with. Half of it is
now implemented in Python and half is not: the Yurdakul critical value has two
independent implementations as of 2026-09-04, and the Population Resemblance
Statistic, the effect-size test and the overlapping statistic have none.

## The gap, stated so it can be falsified

> Every published evaluation of tabular foundation models on credit PD reports
> discrimination and, at most, a pooled calibration scalar. None of them
> measures what a bank's model-acceptance process measures — a trajectory
> across origination vintages, calibration decomposed rather than pooled, and
> population stability against correct critical values.

**Amended 2026-09-04, and narrowed rather than weakened.** The statement used to
say the published evaluations "are in-time: they rank models on randomly drawn
folds". That is true of arXiv:2605.18147 and it is not true in general.
BeyondArena (arXiv:2606.30410) runs a rolling-origin protocol over 142 datasets
including two credit ones, and Drift-Resilient TabPFN (arXiv:2411.10634) has
been the reference for TFMs under temporal shift since NeurIPS 2024. What
survives is a claim about the *measurement* rather than about the split, and it
is checkable one paper at a time: arXiv:2605.18147 has calibration scalars on
random folds; arXiv:2605.18635 has one temporal window and no calibration by
design; arXiv:2606.30410 has many temporal windows and ROC AUC alone, aggregated
into Elo; Mission Lane has proprietary scale and ROC AUC alone. Not one reports
a reliability curve, a score decomposition, or a population-stability statistic
on a credit PD task.

**Amended 2026-09-14, on the calibration leg and on what exists outside
papers.** The sentence above holds for published evaluations of TFMs on credit
PD. Three things now sit beside it. arXiv:2605.18696 reports the reliability
component of the Brier decomposition for six TFMs, pooled over a
random-split suite that contains four credit datasets and reported for none of
them. One public repository, `alexquant1993/tfm_pd_comparison`, commits files
named as reliability curves for TabPFN v2, TabICL and TabDPT on German Credit
under ten-fold cross-validation. On the classical side, arXiv:2604.02351
publishes an annual trajectory of ROC AUC, ECE and Brier for XGBoost on Lending
Club, 2010 to 2018, and the abstract of its SSRN sibling adds PSI. Leg 2 is
therefore stated as: no calibration decomposition, and no calibration
measurement of any kind, for a TFM on a time-ordered credit split. Leg 1 is
stated as: no vintage trajectory for a TFM on credit; the trajectory itself is
published for a GBM. Leg 3 is unchanged. A fourth item bears on the temperature
finding rather than on the legs: the README of
`ulasyurtsever/tabpfn-calibration-shift` states, for TabPFN and TabICL on
census shifts, that temperature scaling cannot repair the loss of calibration
and that an intercept recovers most of it.

**Amended 2026-09-25, on leg 2.** The second half of the 2026-09-14 wording is
false for a tabular foundation model in general. FinTFM, a single-author model
pretrained only on synthetic tables and deposited on Zenodo as software,
scores its hazard head out of time on a random sample of the V4FinBench
corporate panel, trained on company-years through 2016 and scored on those
from 2017. At horizons 0 to 3 it reports a ten-bin expected calibration error
and the mean predicted default rate beside the observed one for its own model,
and the expected calibration error of a per-horizon logistic regression beside
it. It is one cut on the observation year, with no other foundation model
scored. Leg 2 is now stated as: no calibration decomposition, no reliability
curve and no fitted calibration intercept or slope for any tabular foundation
model on a time-ordered credit split; and no calibration measurement of any
kind on such a split for TabPFN or TabICL in any version, or for LimiX-2M,
LimiX-2, Google TabFM or Mitra-v2, the rest of the Models table above. A level
reading out of time, the mean prediction against the observed rate, is public
for a tabular foundation model on credit, so observed over expected out of
time is not new in itself. Legs 1 and 3 are unchanged: FinTFM has no
trajectory and no stability reading.

**Verified against the strongest paper in the set, and it survives — narrower
and sharper than first stated.**

The first version of this statement said the credit TFM papers "report AUC".
That was read off an abstract and it is wrong: arXiv:2605.18147 reports Brier
and LogLoss alongside AUC, Gini, KS and AP. Calibration is not ignored. What is
absent is any temporal axis at all, and any decomposition of those scalars.

The sharper form has three legs:

1. **The split is random.** Five-fold cross-validation over a book that spans
   years trains on 2022 and tests on 2019. Every ranking in that paper is an
   in-time ranking, and in-time ranking is not what a model is accepted on.
2. **Calibration is pooled.** Brier and LogLoss are scalars that mix
   discrimination with calibration. A model can improve Brier while its
   reliability curve gets worse in the segments that carry the lending
   decision. The decomposition is the measurement; the scalar is a summary of
   it.
3. **Stability is not measured, and the paper says it should be.** Its own
   introduction names what regulators require — "the discriminatory power and
   calibration of scorecards, for example through backtesting, monitoring
   stability, and validation requirements" — and then evaluates on random
   folds. The gap is stated by the field's strongest group, in their own
   words, in their own introduction.

One methodological detail worth carrying into the baseline gate: tuned
competitors got up to 20 Optuna trials per fold; the TFMs are zero-shot. That
is a real asymmetry in both directions and it has to be stated in any
comparison this project makes.

### Both papers now verified, and the two of them do not add up to the gap

They fail in complementary directions, which is the strongest position this
project could be in — neither can be cited to close the other's hole.

| | arXiv:2605.18147 | arXiv:2605.18635 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Split | random 5-fold | temporal on LC: train ≤ Jun 2019, test H2 2019 — a window this paper's own cited dataset cannot fill, see below |
| Test windows | n/a | **one**, six months |
| Calibration | Brier, LogLoss, pooled | **none, by design** |
| Reliability curve / decomposition | no | no |
| Population stability | no | no |
| Drift across vintages | no | no |

So: the paper with the metrics has no time axis, and the paper with the time
axis has no metrics. Neither has more than one test window, so neither can say
anything about degradation, which is the thing a risk committee asks first.

One further thing about that single window, and it is a measurement rather than
a reading. arXiv:2605.18635 cites `wordsforthewise/lending-club` on Kaggle as
its reference [19]. In `accepted_2007_to_2018Q4.csv.gz` from that dataset, no
column carries a single value between 2019-07-01 and 2019-12-31: eleven columns
parse as dates, the latest value in any of them is 2019-06-01, and that column
is 99.5% null. A test window of H2 2019 is therefore empty on the origination
axis, on every performance column and on every credit-history column. The split
as described cannot be built from the file as cited —
[`experiments/2026-09-04-lc-vintage-structure`](../../experiments/2026-09-04-lc-vintage-structure)
and [EXP-001](../experiments/EXP-001-lending-club-vintage-structure.md).

What was built instead is not established. At least three readings remain open:
a different release of the same lender's book, published elsewhere and reaching
further; a cut applied to a column that is not an origination date; or a split
defined on something other than loan dates. Nothing here selects between them,
and no statement about that paper's protocol goes beyond this paragraph until
something does.

**And the LC window stops in December 2019.** The largest regime shift in
consumer credit in the dataset's history begins about ten weeks later. A method
whose published temporal validation ends immediately before the stress event is
validated on the easy half. Testing across 2020 is not an extension of that
work; it is the test that work did not run.

### The two methodology papers, read, and what they hand over

Neither closes the gap: one is regression, the other is generic tabular data
capped at ten thousand rows, and neither has a time axis.

ScoringBench supplies no code this project can call. Its scoring rules are
built for continuous outcomes and its dataset filter removes binary targets by
construction, so the suite has never been run against a label of the kind
credit PD produces. What it hands over is protocol. Propriety in the sense of
Gneiting and Raftery, sharpness reported subject to calibration rather than
beside it, the rule that correlated scores are collapsed to one number per
subject before anything is compared across subjects, and the habit of pairing
an ordinal ranking with a magnitude-preserving one. The collapse rule reaches
this project directly: per-vintage scores on one dataset are repeated measures
on one subject, exactly as per-fold scores are. Consequences in
[ADR-0003](../decisions/ADR-0003-scoring-rules-for-a-binary-label.md).

The uncertainty benchmark hands over the sharpest published statement of the
reliability axis on tabular data, and its limits describe this project's
setting. Its ranking on AUC is the published ranking; its ranking on
conditional coverage inverts it, and a logistic regression finishes first. That
is the ordering this project tests on a time axis, on credit data, at scale, on
metrics a model-acceptance process names.
